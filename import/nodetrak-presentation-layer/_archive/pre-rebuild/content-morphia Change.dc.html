Change and status

A node re-aggregates on a cadence and is never deleted, so a reader arriving at one has to be told which state it is in and what has moved since. Four components carry that: the state notice, the index change log, the correction and the limitation register.

ss: 01

State on a node

The six status chips are already closed. What this section fixes is what each one means for a node in the index, and what the node must say to a reader who lands on it in that state. The served state says nothing at all: the absence of a notice is how a reader knows they are reading the current aggregation.
Nothing is removed. A deprecated or archived node keeps its address because other nodes point at it, and each one names its successor or states that there is none.

ss: 02

The index change log

One node's history is a diff between two of its aggregations, and the version selector already carries it. This is the other view: every change across the index, newest first, so a reader who has been away can see which nodes moved without opening them.
A row exists only where the meaning changed. Re-wording that leaves a claim intact is not a change, and a log full of those hides the two rows that matter.

ss: 03

Correction

A correction is the one change that is never made silently. Someone acted on the wrong figure, so the node has to say what it said, what is true, and what depended on it. It appears twice: in place, where the error was, and as a permanent dated row in the node's history.
The test against a revision is a single question. Was the earlier statement wrong when it was published? Then it is a correction. If it was right and the world moved, it is a revision, and the diff already carries it.

In place, where the error was

Correction 12 Aug 2026
This section gave the tier-two freshness window as 12 hours. The agreed window is 6 hours, and has been since the tiering was set in March. Any table assessed against the 12-hour figure between 2 and 12 August was assessed too leniently; the seven affected tables are named in the review note.
Four things in order: what was said, what is true, the period the wrong figure was live, and what depended on it. The date is the date of the correction, not of the error — the error's date is inside the third clause.

The row stays for the life of the node and survives every later aggregation. A revision sits in the same list and reads as an ordinary change, which is the distinction the reader needs: one of these two rows means the node was wrong.
ss: 04

Known limitations

A limitation is what the node cannot tell you, stated so the reader stops looking for it. One limitation is a callout at the point a reader would expect the answer. Past three, the node carries a register instead, because four callouts in a document read as four separate alarms.
Limitation
Freshness is measured at table level and never per partition, because three of the five ingest paths emit no partition timestamp. A partitioned table that is current in one partition and a week behind in another reads as current here.
