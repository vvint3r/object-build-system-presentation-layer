Power, sample size and duration

How many units an experiment needs before it can tell a real effect from noise, how long that takes on the traffic actually available, and what the answer costs when the design is not the clean one the formula assumes. Sized throughout on one anchor: new-customer activation on the onboarding surface.
Published Method 14 min · reviewed Aug 2026 · owner experimentation pod
ss: 01

What sizing decides

A power calculation is a commitment device. Before any exposure you name four quantities, and the sample size follows from them. None of it is recoverable afterwards: power is a property of a design, not of a dataset, so it cannot be computed back into an experiment that has already run.
Everything below is sized on one anchor, so that the numbers compare. Where a figure appears without a unit, it is units per arm.

ss: 02

Four dials, one equation

An experiment sees the true effect plus random noise, and whether it can separate them depends on how wide the noise is relative to the effect. Every symbol below is a way of saying that. The four questions people put to a sizing calculator are this one equation rearranged.
n = (z_(1−α/2) + z_(1−β))² · V / δ²

What the design costs on top of the formula

Four properties of a real design multiply the base figure: clustering, allocation, arm count and triggering. Each is a factor, so they compound. Only the last one is large here, and it is large because effect enters the equation squared.

Allocation is the one factor teams reach for first and it is the weakest lever available. For fixed total traffic the variance of the arm difference is minimised at an even split, so an uneven split r costs exactly 1/(4r(1−r)) times the balanced total.
ss: 04

The registered timeline

The quotient of sample over weekly traffic is the shortest a test can be, never the length it will run. Three floors apply and the tallest wins: the calculated duration, whole weekly cycles so that every weekday is represented equally, and the time the metric itself needs to mature.

Registered for the activation test

Exposure runs five whole weeks and ends on the weekday it began. The reporting date sits fourteen days later, because the last cohort has to be given the same fourteen-day window as the first.

ss: 05

Reading a null

Two nulls look identical on a dashboard and are opposite findings. Separating them means running the sizing equation backwards on the sample actually collected, which is rarely the sample that was registered.
δ_achieved = (z_(1−α/2) + z_(1−β)) · √(V / n_realised)
Compare the result against the effect the decision needed. That comparison, not the p-value, is what separates the two readings:

An informative null

The achieved MDE is at or below the effect the decision required. The test could have seen a difference worth acting on and did not. Ship the decision that follows from no effect.

An uninformative null

The achieved MDE is above it. The test never had the resolution to answer the question, so the result carries no information about the effect and must not be reported as evidence of none.
Limitation Observed power — power recomputed from the effect the test happened to measure — is the mirror-image temptation and carries no information at all. It is a monotone restatement of the p-value, so it can never tell you anything the p-value did not.

Separately from getting it wrong, there are places the calculation cannot help. It needs a variance measured on the metric, at the randomisation unit, over a representative window. Where the metric is new or the surface has never been instrumented, none of those exists, and running the formula on a plausible-looking figure produces a guess wearing the costume of arithmetic.
Note The honest sequence in that case is a pilot to estimate variance — not an A/A test, whose job is validating assignment — and then a design. The same applies wherever the eligible population cannot be counted in advance.
And one claim is almost always overstated. "We powered the test" nearly always means the primary metric was powered at the overall level. Guardrails, secondary metrics and segment reads carry their own requirements, and those are much larger.
