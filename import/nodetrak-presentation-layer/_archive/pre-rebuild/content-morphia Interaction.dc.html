Interaction in place

Five controls that change what is visible without changing where the reader is. Nothing here fetches, submits or mutates a node: each one is view state over material the page already holds, which is why every one of them must state what it is hiding.
Components 5 components · 8 rules · specimens run on the certification sample
ss: 01

Disclosure

A disclosure trades certainty for length: the reader accepts a shorter page in exchange for trusting the header to describe what is folded away. That trust is the whole component, so the header states the contents — a count, a date, a threshold — not a teaser.
It follows that only skippable material collapses. Three peer policies, each read on its own, are the correct case. A sequence is not: if the reader needs the third section to follow the fourth, folding it hides the argument rather than the detail.

A backfill rewrites history, so it suspends the freshness measure for the partitions it touches rather than repairing them. The measure resumes at the next scheduled run, and the suspended window is stated on the metric node instead of being smoothed away.
Backfills longer than seven days require the owning team to re-run certification, because a rewrite of that size changes the reconciliation baseline the gates test against.
Two feeds hold standing exemptions from the freshness gate: a weekly vendor extract that cannot arrive daily, and a partner file whose delivery window is contractual. Both carry an expiry date, and an expired exemption fails the gate like any other breach.
No exemption is granted for tier 1. A tier-1 table that cannot meet the threshold is re-tiered, which is a decision about the table rather than about the measure.
Header = marker + T3 title + the contents fact. Sections are peers on one surface, split by inset hairlines. The marker never carries meaning the words do not.
ss: 02

Sorting and filtering

Sorting reorders a table; filtering shortens it. The reader can see the first and cannot see the second, so a filtered table states its own count against the whole and names every filter in force. A sort states its key and its direction in words, because a mark on a column head tells the reader which column and not which way.

ss: 03

Overview and detail

A node with a dozen context nodes attached cannot show them all at reading length, and a list of titles alone will not tell the reader which one answers the question. The pair solves it: the overview stays visible and marks the selection, the detail reads at full length beside it.
The detail is a peer surface, not an overlay. An overlay would cover the list the reader is comparing against, which is the one thing this component exists to prevent.

ss: 04

Definitions in place

A term of art has a node of its own, and sending the reader to it mid-sentence costs them their place. The definition panel gives one sentence and the way through to the node — a definition, never an explanation. Two paragraphs in a panel means the term deserved a link.

A defined term takes the same teal underline as a link and no second marker. One panel is open at a time, and the panel holds one link and its close control — nothing else is interactive inside it.

ss: 06

Rules added

Eight rules, binding on any surface in this system that hides, reorders or defers material.
R24.1
Disclosure hides peers, never a sequence. If the reader needs the folded section to follow the next one, it does not fold. Numbered steps, arguments and anything a later section depends on stay open.
R24.2
A disclosure header states its contents, not a promise. A count, a date, a threshold or a verdict — the fact that lets the reader skip it safely. The header is a real button with its own T3 heading and aria-expanded; peers share one surface split by inset hairlines.
R24.3
A sort states key and direction in words. One sort at a time. The active column takes a 2px teal underline; the direction is written out beside the row count, because a mark on a column head names the column and not the order.
R24.4
A filtered table states its count against the whole. Filters sit above the table, never inside the header row, and every filter in force is named. An empty result keeps the controls and says what would restore the rows.
R24.5
Detail is a peer surface, never an overlay. The overview stays visible and marks the selection; the detail names it again in its own heading. The two surfaces take different depth families — a dashed canvas of lifted rows against a hairline card — so the pair never reads as two competing cards.
R24.6
A definition panel defines; it does not explain. One sentence, the source count, and one link to the node. Two paragraphs means the term needed a link instead of a panel.
R24.7
One panel open at a time, at overlay depth. A defined term carries the link marker and no second marker. The panel holds its one link and a close control; nothing else inside it is interactive.
R24.8
A trail carries the delta or says there is none. Recently viewed is ordered by recency, capped at five, and every row states what changed on that node since the visit. Without the delta it is a bookmark list, which this product does not have.
