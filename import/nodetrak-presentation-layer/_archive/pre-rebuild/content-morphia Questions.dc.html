Questions and answers

Four components for material a reader arrives with rather than reads into: the question-answer pair, the FAQ list, the correction of something commonly said, and the table of two things that get mistaken for each other.
Components Q&A family Four components · six rules
ss: 01

The question-answer pair

A question earns its place by having been asked. It is written the way the reader asked it, including the assumption behind it, and the answer resolves in its first sentence — everything after that is qualification for the reader who needs it.

The pair, expanded

Why did my table lose its certificate when nothing changed?
Because certification expires on its own. A certificate lasts ninety days from the review that granted it, and it lapses whether or not the table changed — the review is what expired, not the data.
Re-issuing is the same review, usually inside a day where nothing has moved. The catalog shows the expiry date on every certified table, and the owner is notified two weeks before it arrives.
Question at 17px/800, answer at body. The first sentence names the cause; the second paragraph is the reader’s next move. No heading above the question, because the question is the heading.

The same material, written badly

Certificate lifecycle and expiry policy
Certification operates on a fixed-term basis. The platform team introduced expiry in 2025 following a review of catalog trust, and the current term reflects the cadence agreed with domain owners at that time.
A topic where a question was asked, and history where an answer was needed. The reader who typed the question into search does not match this heading, and the first sentence resolves nothing.

ss: 03

Commonly said, actually true

A correction is one row with two halves, never two lists. Split into a list of wrong beliefs and a list of right ones, a reader has to pair them up themselves and will pair some of them wrongly. The claim is quoted as people say it, without mockery, because the reader may be the one who said it.
