Selection

The three closed lists an implementer works from: which labels exist, which component the material calls for, and what must be true before the page ships. A label outside ss: 01 is not a label. A component chosen against ss: 02 is the wrong component even when it renders correctly. ss: 03 is run against finished output, not read before starting.
ss: 01

Label vocabulary

Three closed lists. Eight callouts, six status chips, three requirement levels — seventeen labels, and no eighteenth. Do and Do not sit outside the count: they are paired-list markers, legal only in that shape. Each callout carries the nearest label it is not, because every wrong label in practice has been a near miss rather than a wild one. Fill intensity follows the tint rule: tint by default, one solid verdict per view.

Status chips — six, in one order

A surface carries at most one. A title block carries at most three chips of any kind; aliases past four collapse behind a disclosure.
Draft In review Approved Published Deprecated Archived

Requirement levels — three

Applied to a field, a step or a condition. A requirement that is not one of these three is a status chip and belongs in the row above.

ss: 02

Component selection matrix

Every shipped component, with the condition that selects it, the condition that disqualifies it, what it may contain and what it may not. Read the shape of the source material, never its subject: a page about a process does not earn a process diagram, material that is ordered steps does. Rows marked Not built name the substitute to use until they ship.

ss: 03

Pre-flight gate

Run against finished output, in order, counting before judging. Every item names a number or a boolean, so a pass is a fact rather than an opinion. A page that fails any item in passes one to three does not ship; passes four to six are corrected in place.
