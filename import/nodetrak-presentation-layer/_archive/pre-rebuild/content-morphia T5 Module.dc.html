ss: 3p-values and significance
Your test ends and the dashboard says p = .003. Before you write "significant" in the memo, be precise about what that number is: the probability of seeing a difference this large if the change actually did nothing. It is a statement about the data under the null — not the probability your variant works.
Definitionalso: significance level
p-value
The probability of observing an effect at least as extreme as the one measured, assuming the null hypothesis is true.
p = P( |Δ| ≥ Δ_observed | H₀ )
Two consequences. First, a small p-value with a tiny effect can be statistically significant and commercially irrelevant — significance is not size. Second, checking p daily multiplies your chances of a false positive; that is why the lifecycle seals the primary metric until the end date.

A test returns p = .003. Which statement is correct?
AThere is a 99.7% chance the variant is better
B ✓Data this extreme would occur 0.3% of the time if the change did nothingCORRECT
CThe effect is large enough to matter commercially
WhyA p-value conditions on the null being true; it says nothing about effect size or the probability the variant "works". Live single-choice behavior is specced in Structured ss: 09.

Summary

Three things to keep
A p-value is about the data under the null — never about your variant's probability of being good.
Significance ≠ size. Report the effect and its interval, not just the star.
Peeking inflates false positives — seal the primary until the end date.
