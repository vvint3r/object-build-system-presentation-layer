Choosing a stats engine

Three inference approaches for the experimentation platform's default analysis, compared against the criteria that decided the FY27 rollout.
Comparison Experimentation Decision record ~10 min · updated Aug 2026

What's being compared

The platform rollout needs one default engine every test gets unless someone argues otherwise. Candidates: fixed-horizon frequentist, Bayesian, and sequential (mSPRT). Criteria are weighted toward peeking safety and decision speed — most historical damage came from stopping tests early, not from the math.

The options

Option A
Fixed-horizon frequentist
Classic t-test at a pre-committed sample size.
Strengths
+Universally understood readout
+Cheapest infra, zero new tooling
Tradeoffs
−Peeking inflates false positives
−Waits full horizon even on blowouts
Best for · regulated, one-shot analyses
Option B
Bayesian
Posterior probability that treatment beats control.
Strengths
+Intuitive "P(better)" statement
+Workable on small samples with priors
Tradeoffs
−Prior choice becomes the argument
−Heaviest compute + education cost
Best for · low-traffic B2B surfaces
Option C
Sequential — mSPRT
Always-valid inference; stop whenever the bound crosses.
Strengths
+Peek any time — validity holds
+30–50% faster on clear winners
Tradeoffs
−Wider intervals early in the test
−Fewer textbook references for analysts
Best for · platform default, high traffic

Verdict

Decision ADR-014 · accepted
Sequential (mSPRT) becomes the platform default
Kills the peeking problem structurally — no analyst discipline required
Median test 9 days shorter in the 12-test pilot backtest
Readout stays a familiar confidence interval — lowest retraining cost
Decided Jul 2026 · review Jan 2027 · owner · experimentation guild

When to deviate

Regulatory or partner reporting. Use fixed-horizon frequentist with the α documented before launch — auditors want the pre-commitment, not the speed.
Tiny samples with strong priors. Bayesian, with the prior written into the test plan and reviewed by the guild before data arrives.
If the platform adds bandits. Re-open ADR-014 — adaptive allocation and inference couple, and the default must be re-argued.
