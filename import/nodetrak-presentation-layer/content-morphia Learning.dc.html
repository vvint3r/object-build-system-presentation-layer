Four components a teaching surface needs beyond prose: the three checks that are not single choice, the summary that closes a module, the cycle that repeats on a cadence, and the model that names an abstraction along with the condition that breaks it.

A check tests one stated objective, and the intent selects its shape. Single choice covers one right answer and lives with the learning components in Structured. The three below cover the rest: a set of answers, a claim, and a question with no answer at all. There is no fifth shape.

{{ tfMark }}
Certification is time-boxed. It expires ninety days after issue whether or not a check has failed, and re-issue requires the whole check set to pass again.

Reflection

Not scored
Use where the question has no single answer. A reflection never gates the module and never reports a result.
Your team owns forty tables and can certify six a quarter. Which six do you take first, and what makes the other thirty-four acceptable to leave?
A reference answer
Rank by consumers against decision latency: a table read by a contract feed outranks a larger one read at quarterly review. The other thirty-four are acceptable when each carries a named owner and a declared freshness expectation — an uncertified table with those two facts is legible, and without them it is unreadable whether certified or not.
The block that closes a module. It restates the objectives declared at the top in the completed tense, says where each was covered and how it was assessed, and names the boundary. It introduces nothing: a statement here that appears nowhere above is a gap in the module, not a line in the summary.

A named abstraction the reader is meant to carry away and apply. Four parts are mandatory: the schematic, the parts it names, the relationship that binds them, and the condition under which it breaks. The last one is what separates a model from a diagram, and it is the part most often left out.

The two costs are read independently, and neither can be inferred from the other. Where either is unknown the model returns no placement, and the unknown cost is itself the finding.
It holds when both costs are estimable within an order of magnitude, and the table serves one class of consumer.
It breaks when one table serves a dashboard and a contract feed. Two costs of staleness means two placements, so the table is split before it is placed.
