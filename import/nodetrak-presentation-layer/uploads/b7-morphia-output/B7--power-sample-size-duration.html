Questions this document answers

Seven questions, each with its short answer and a jump link.

How many units does an experiment need, and where does the formula come from?

One equation — n = (z1−α/2+z1−β)²·V/δ² — with four inputs you name before exposure. On Mirocart's activation surface it returns 135,920 per arm.

Where should the minimum detectable effect actually come from?

From the profit-and-loss, run backwards: build cost plus the cost of being wrong, divided by the annual value of one unit of effect. At Mirocart that division gives τ = 0.51pp.

→ 3. What change matters · → 8. Where the MDE really comes from

What do clusters, unequal splits, extra arms and triggering do to n?

They multiply it, they compound rather than compete, and exactly one design choice — variance reduction — ever divides. Trigger dilution is the one that enters squared.

→ 10. Design multipliers

How long will it run, and which floors can only ever make that longer?

Divide n by the arrival rate, then take the maximum over every floor — weekly cycle, business cycle, maturation, steady state. Never the average, never the sum. On activation: 4.53 weeks calculated, five whole weeks registered.

→ 6. Sample → calendar

The result was not significant — what am I allowed to say?

It depends entirely on the achieved MDE. If it sits at or below τ the null is resolved and the question is closed; if above, the null is unresolved and "it didn't work" is a claim the data cannot support.

What the calculation actually is

Important
A power calculation is a commitment device
Before exposure you name four quantities — α, the power 1−β, the variance V of the outcome on the unit you randomise, and δ, the smallest difference the test must detect — and the sample size follows.
Caution
None of it is recoverable later
Power is a property of a design, not of a dataset. You cannot compute it back into an experiment that has already run.
Key point
The fifth constraint nobody writes down
The traffic budget caps n before the dials are set. That is what makes the exercise a negotiation rather than a calculation.

The same equation, asked four ways

The four questions people put to a sample-size calculator are one equation rearranged. Pick the one you are actually asking.

Mode 1 · solve for n — sizing a test
narm = (z1−α/2 + z1−β)² · V / δ²

In plain words: how many units per arm do I need to be able to see an effect of size δ?

Mirocart: at δ = 0.51pp on activation → 135,920 per arm.

Mode 2 · solve for δ — what a fixed budget can see
δmin = (z1−α/2 + z1−β) · √(V / narm)

In plain words: given the traffic I actually have, what is the smallest effect I could reliably detect?

Mirocart: at 90,000 per arm — three weeks — → 0.627pp, which does not clear τ = 0.51pp.

Mode 3 · solve for power — the odds on an effect you were not sized for
1 − β = Φ( δ / √(V / narm) − z1−α/2 )

In plain words: if the effect really is δ, how often would a design this size actually detect it?

Mirocart: at 90,000 per arm the chance of detecting the 0.51pp effect is 62.5% — a five-in-eight coin flip sold internally as an experiment.

Mode 4 · divide by an arrival rate — a calendar
duration = ntotal / eligible units per day

In plain words: at the rate users actually arrive, how long does collecting that sample take?

Mirocart: 271,840 ÷ 60,000 a week → 4.53 weeks, registered as five.

6 · Sample → calendar

The quotient is the shortest a test can be, never the length it will run

271,840 customers is not a plan. Traffic turns it into weeks — and then four floors turn those weeks into a date.

8 · Deeper — the MDE

Where the MDE really comes from, and what it costs to move it

Section 3 derived δ = 0.51pp from the P&L. This section is the discipline behind that move, the two scales δ can be quoted on, and the ladder that prices every alternative.

Important
Definition
The MDE is the smallest true difference a design can detect with probability 1−β.
Mistake
The antipattern this replaces
Almost every treatment in circulation asserts the MDE — "let's detect a 1% lift" — and asserting it is where sizing goes wrong.

In plain words

Plugged

The two disciplines that fix it

Discipline 1 · derive, don't choose

δ is derived from the profit-and-loss of the decision rather than chosen for roundness. That is the staircase in 3. What change matters.

δ is compared, before launch, against τ — the minimum effect of interest, the smallest result the business would act on.

9 · Deeper — variance

One equation, four variance terms — and the term is chosen by the unit, not the metric

The lab's V assumed a rate. Change the metric and only that one symbol moves — but it is the symbol that goes wrong most often.

Important
There is one sizing formula, not four
The specific equations for proportions, means, survival and correlations all reduce to the same general form [S6]. n_arm = (z1−α/2+z1−β)²·V/δ² covers every metric a product organisation sizes on — whether a rate, a mean, a count or a ratio. Only V changes.

Where the effort actually goes

Correct code is short — and only one line matters

Every value below was executed against statsmodels 0.14.6 [S9].

from scipy.stats import norm
from statsmodels.stats.power import NormalIndPower
from statsmodels.stats.proportion import proportion_effectsize

def n_per_arm(p0, mde_abs, alpha=0.05, power=0.80):   # mde_abs is ABSOLUTE:
    pbar = p0 + mde_abs / 2                           # 0.0051 == 0.51pp,
    k = (norm.ppf(1 - alpha/2) + norm.ppf(power))**2  # never a relative lift
    return k * 2 * pbar * (1 - pbar) / mde_abs**2

sp = NormalIndPower().solve_power                # nobs1, i.e. PER GROUP

   alpha=0.05, power=0.80)                       # h=0.032629 ->  14,744.1

15 · Common pitfalls

Five ways this goes wrong, and what each costs

Each pitfall pairs the mistake with what it costs on the activation design and the correction (facet slot F-MTH-3).

Pitfall 1
The MDE set by convenience rather than by the decision

"Let's detect one percent" is chosen for roundness.

305,820 per arm

Consequence: a ten-week request nobody priced.

Correction: derive δ by dividing build cost plus the cost of being wrong by the annual value of one unit of effect (3).

Pitfall 2
Absolute and relative effects conflated

A brief says "1.5%", a calculator takes an absolute MDE, and nobody notices which was meant.

8.7× under-sized

Consequence: 15,712 per arm instead of 135,920 — and a real win reported as a null.

Correction: size on the absolute scale with the baseline adjacent, converting explicitly whenever a relative figure is quoted.

Pitfall 3
Sizing on a population wider than the change can reach

The treatment touches a step only half of signups see, but the calculation uses every signup.

×4 · 9.06 weeks

Consequence: n rises by the square of the trigger rate — 9.06 weeks instead of 4.53.

Correction: fix the estimand first, then size on the population that matches it (10).

Pitfall 4
Variance borrowed from the wrong metric, unit or window — or clustering ignored

A σ lifted from a similar-looking metric or a promotional fortnight, or a market-randomised test sized on user counts.

DE 6.03

Consequence: both are invisible at launch — at dispatch's 504 windows per market an ICC of 0.01 gives a design effect of 6.03, so a test believed adequately powered holds a sixth of the information it claims.

Correction: recompute V on the exact metric, unit and a recent window (Table 3), and estimate the ICC per primary metric [S7].

Pitfall 5
The calculated duration reported as the duration

The quotient is handed to a stakeholder as an end date.

a mid-week stop

Consequence: a run stopping mid-week, over-weighting weekends, or scoring a 14-day metric on four-day-old cohorts.

Correction: register the maximum over the quotient and every floor (6).

16 · Limits and boundaries

Where this calculation cannot help, and where it lies

Three boundary classes, and then the limitation that applies to every number in this document.

Note
Boundary 1 — when it is simply unachievable
The calculation needs a variance measured on the metric, at the randomisation unit, on a representative window. For a new metric or a never-instrumented surface none of those exists, and running the formula on a plausible-looking σ produces a guess wearing the costume of arithmetic. The honest sequence is a pilot to estimate σ — not an A/A test, whose job is validating assignment (A3:D4) — and then a design. The same applies where the eligible population cannot be counted in advance.
Note
Boundary 2 — when it is falsely claimed
"We powered the test" nearly always means "we powered the primary metric at the overall level". Guardrails, secondary metrics and segment reads carry their own, much larger requirements.
What a quarter-sized segment can actually see
Overall
0.51pp
Quarter-size segment
~1.02pp

Because n falls by four and precision moves as 1/√n, the detectable effect doubles. A test sized for the headline cannot support the segment table beside it — and that table is usually where the decision is made.

Note
Boundary 3 — when a stronger method makes things worse
Driving δ towards what the business "really" wants can push the registered duration past the window in which the answer is still true. The 0.25pp row of Table 2 is a nineteen-week design — statistically impeccable, and an answer about a market that no longer exists when it lands.
Limitation
Key limitation
Every number this unit produces is conditional on a variance and a baseline measured before the experiment, on a population defined before it, for a single metric — and none of those three survives contact with a launch unchanged.
The claim
A power calculation is a forecast, and the only forecast in an experiment that nobody revisits: the realised V sits in the results table, and almost no organisation compares it to the one it planned with.

Allocation ratioThe planned share of traffic per arm; balance is optimal under equal variances and a split r costs 1/(4r(1−r)).Ramped allocation (A3:B5) — the penalty attaches to the realised split over the analysed window, not the launch-day split
Design effectDE = 1+(m̄−1)ρ_ICC, the factor by which clustering inflates the required sample [S7].The effective sample n_eff = n/DE: DE multiplies when sizing, n_eff deflates at analysis
Intra-cluster correlation ρ_ICCShare of a metric's variance attributable to cluster membership; re-estimated per primary metric.The CUPED covariate correlation ρ (A3:C5), same symbol, opposite effect — ρ_ICC inflates n, CUPED's ρ shrinks it
Power dilutionLoss of detectable effect from analysing a population wider than the change can reach: δ_ITT = π·δ_triggered, so n ∝ 1/π².Low power generally — dilution is specific, quadratic and repaired by changing the analysis population
Readout
Achieved MDEThe MDE a test actually had, (z1−α/2+z1−β)√(V/n_realised), at the realised sample. Every null readout carries it.The planned MDE on a forecast sample; the two diverge whenever traffic, eligibility or variance missed their estimates
Observed (post-hoc) powerPower recomputed at the observed effect — a monotone re-expression of the p-value that adds nothing [S3].Planned power, fixed before launch; and conditional power (A3:C6), forward-looking and used for futility
Resolved vs unresolved nullResolved when the achieved MDE is at or below τ, so what matters is ruled out; unresolved when above τ, so nothing was."Not significant", which describes both — identical on a dashboard, opposite findings (Table 5)
Equivalence test (TOST)Two one-sided tests placing an effect inside ±τ — "prove it isn't there"; ~148,300 per arm here.A non-significant superiority test, which says nothing about size unless the achieved MDE clears τ
Type S and Type M errorAt low power a significant result has a raised chance of the wrong sign (S) and an inflated magnitude (M) — 2.25× at 20% power [S4].Type I and Type II error (A3:C1), about whether you reject; S and M about how wrong the estimate is
Winner's curse at low powerSelection on significance in an under-powered design publishes inflated effects, so the portfolio under-delivers.The winner's curse at sequential boundaries (A3:C6); and portfolio selection bias (A3:E3)
Duration and regime boundaries
Duration floorAny constraint that can only extend a run — weekly cycle, business cycle, maturation, steady state; the registered duration is their maximum.The calculated duration n_total ÷ eligible units per day, a lower bound and never an end date
Metric maturation windowThe time one unit's outcome needs to become observable; M5 scores a first order within 14 days of signup.Sample accrual, which speeds up with traffic — maturation floors the reporting date
Fixed-horizon designA design analysed exactly once at a pre-committed sample size — the only condition under which α is the realised error rate.A sequential design (A3:C6), analysed at several priced points, registering n_max not n_fixed
Inflation factor RThe multiplier turning n_fixed into a sequential design's n_max: 1.0196 for LD-OBF at K = 4. B7 applies it and never derives it.The boundary values and spending function that produce it, which are A3:C6's and appear nowhere here

30-second recall

The whole document as six tiles, two closers, and two checklists built to be printed.

The equation

n_arm = (z1−α/2+z1−β)²V/δ² — four dials, three free, traffic decides which.

MDE

Derive δ from the P&L, state it absolute beside its baseline, compare to τ before launch; the absolute/relative conflation costs 8.7×.

Variance

One formula, four V terms, chosen by the randomisation unit before the metric's name.

Multipliers

Clustering, split, arm count and trigger dilution multiply, dilution quadratically; only variance reduction divides.

Duration

Divide, then take the maximum over every floor.

Nulls

Every null carries its achieved MDE, and observed power is the p-value in a costume.

Mistake
Biggest mistake
An MDE chosen for roundness.
Signal
Interview phrase
"The product is not the number, it is the trade-off stated before exposure."

Five failure patterns

δ set by convenience, then defended by convention.
"1.5%" entered as 1.5 percentage points.
The whole population sized for a change half of it cannot see.
A design effect omitted on a market- or account-randomised test.
A null reported as "no effect" with no achieved MDE beside it.
