First response time for new agents

Part 01

Purpose

New Tier 1 agents take this module to read first response time the way the quarterly review reports it. Their replies stop its clock, and the quarter's figure is held to a target.

Part 02

Objectives

  1. Work out a ticket's first response time from its timestamps: lesson 1, exercise 1.
  2. Report a period's first response time as the median of its tickets: lesson 2, exercise 2.
  3. Tell first response time apart from specialist queue wait and resolution within SLA: lesson 3, exercise 3.
Part 03

Prerequisites

The lessons explain the measure, not the helpdesk itself.

  • Helpdesk onboarding done, so macros, internal notes and escalations are familiar.
  • The support operations glossary open alongside, for the exact sense of each term.
Part 04

Lessons

Each lesson states a rule, then works a case through it.

  1. Find the clock's start and stop

    The clock starts at the ticket's creation time. It stops at the first public reply a person sends, including a reply sent with a macro.S1

    Automatic acknowledgements and internal notes do not stop it. The clock runs through nights and weekends, since it measures the wait the customer sees.

    Outcome

    Every timestamp on a ticket sorts into clock start, clock stop or neither.

  2. Take the median across the period

    A period's first response time is the median of the tickets created in it, not the mean. Tickets created by staff, spam and merged duplicates are left out first.S1

    Five tickets of 7m, 45m, 1h 50m, 3h 40m and 43h 30m have a median of 1h 50m. Their mean is 9h 58m, pulled up by one weekend ticket.

    Outcome

    A list of ticket times reduces to one median, with the left-out tickets removed first.

  3. Separate first response from the other measures

    First response time stops at the first reply, including the reply that follows an escalation. Specialist queue wait runs from the escalation until a specialist picks the ticket up, and also counts every hour.

    Resolution within SLA is a test, not a wait: the ticket passes when it is resolved before its due time. Chat and phone get 4 hours; email, web form and social get 24 business hours. Business hours run 08:00 to 18:00 on weekdays, in the time zone of the Tier 1 region that takes the ticket.S2

    The measures can disagree: in Q3, support met its first response target and missed its resolution target.S3

    Outcome

    An escalated ticket gives three readings: a first response time, a specialist queue wait and a due time met or missed.

Part 05

Exercises

Try each exercise before opening its answer; every answer shows its working. The tickets are illustrative.

Chat with an acknowledgement and a note

A chat opens at 09:15 and gets an automatic acknowledgement at once. An agent adds an internal note at 09:18, then sends the first public reply with a macro at 09:26. Work out the chat's first response time.

Answer

Exercise 1

The answer is 11m. The clock starts at 09:15 and stops at the 09:26 macro reply; neither the acknowledgement nor the internal note stops it.

Six tickets in one week

Six tickets created in one week waited 5m, 12m, 35m, 1h 05m, 2h 20m and 26h for a first reply. A staff member created the 5m ticket. Work out the week's first response time.

Answer

Exercise 2

The answer is 1h 05m. The staff ticket is left out, and 1h 05m is the middle of the five times left. Their mean would be 6h 02m, pulled up by the 26h ticket.

Escalated billing email

A billing email arrives at 15:10 on a Wednesday, and at 15:40 a Tier 1 agent escalates it, then replies. A specialist picks it up at 09:10 on Thursday and resolves it at 11:00 on Friday. Give the first response time and specialist queue wait, and say whether the ticket met its due time.

Answer

Exercise 3

First response time is 30m, from 15:10 to the 15:40 reply. Specialist queue wait is 17h 30m, from the 15:40 escalation to 09:10 on Thursday. The due time falls 24 business hours after 15:10 on Wednesday, at 09:10 on Monday, so the ticket was resolved within SLA.S2

Part 06

Exceptions and recovery

Two cases make the measure understate the customer's wait.

Merged ticket

A merged ticket keeps the creation time of the ticket that survives the merge.S1 When the customer first wrote on the duplicate, the measured time is shorter than the real wait. Check the merge history before quoting such a ticket's time.

Ticket still waiting for a reply

A ticket with no reply yet has no first response time, so a median leaves out the longest waits. A quarter's figure is fixed at the first warehouse refresh after it closes, and a ticket still waiting then stays out.S1 Quote a figure with the count of tickets still waiting beside it.
