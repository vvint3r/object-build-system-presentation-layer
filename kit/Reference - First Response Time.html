First response time

The wait from a ticket's creation to its first human reply, as Support Operations measures and targets it.

Part 01

Definition

First response time is how long a customer waits for a person to answer a new ticket. Support Operations reports it each quarter and judges routing and staffing changes against it.

Part 02

Properties

The metric has one owner, one source system and a quarterly target.

Metric

First response time

The time from a ticket's creation to the first public reply from an agent, counted through nights and weekends. A period reports the median across the tickets created in it. Automatic acknowledgements do not stop the clock.

Part 04

Measurement

Four clock rules set each ticket's time. The median keeps a few very long waits from moving the reported value, as they would move a mean.

Definitions
Clock start
The ticket's creation time, created_at, in UTC. A merged ticket keeps the creation time of the ticket that survives the merge.
Clock stop
The first public reply from an agent, first_human_reply_at. Replies sent with a macro count; automatic acknowledgements and internal notes do not.1
Excluded tickets
Tickets created by internal staff, tickets marked as spam, and merged duplicates. None of them has a customer waiting for a reply.
Reported value
The median across tickets created in the period, in hours and minutes. A quarter's value comes from the first warehouse refresh after the quarter closes.
frt_median.sqlSQL
-- median first response time, in minutes, for tickets created in Q3 2026
select
  percentile_cont(0.5) within group (
    order by datediff('minute', created_at, first_human_reply_at)
  ) as frt_median_min
from support.tickets_fact
where created_at >= '2026-07-01' and created_at < '2026-10-01'
  and is_spam = false
  and is_merged_duplicate = false
  and created_by_staff = false
  and first_human_reply_at is not null
Part 05

Relationships

First response time connects to three metrics in the quarterly review, and the billing escalation guide sets the reply that stops it.

  • MetricResolved within SLA

    Measures the whole ticket against its due time, so a fast first reply alone does not meet it.

  • MetricSpecialist queue wait

    Starts when a ticket is escalated, often after the first reply has stopped this clock.

  • MetricCustomer satisfaction

    Rates the whole ticket, from a survey sent 24 hours after it closes.S4

  • GuideBilling ticket escalation to specialists

    Step 6 sends the reply that stops the clock on an escalated billing ticket.

Part 06

Examples

Each example applies the clock rules to concrete tickets. The tickets and their times are illustrative; the APAC medians come from the Q3 review.

Chat with an automatic acknowledgement

A customer opens a chat at 10:02 and gets an automatic acknowledgement at once. An agent sends the first reply at 10:09. First response time is 7m, since the acknowledgement does not stop the clock.

Email over a weekend

An email arrives at 14:00 on a Saturday, and an agent replies at 09:30 on Monday. First response time is 43h 30m, because the clock runs through the weekend.

The same rule shapes APAC's Q3 result. Its median was 3h 20m with unstaffed weekends counted, and 2h 31m on weekdays only.S4

Escalated billing ticket

A billing email arrives at 11:20 on a Tuesday, and a Tier 1 agent replies at 12:05 while escalating it. First response time is 45m. The wait for a billing specialist after that reply counts as specialist queue wait.

Median of five tickets

Five tickets have first response times of 7m, 45m, 1h 50m, 3h 40m and 43h 30m. The median is 1h 50m, the middle value; the mean is 9h 58m, pulled up by the weekend ticket. A sixth ticket, created by a staff member, is left out.
