How long customers wait for a reply, a resolution and a specialist, and which documents cover each.

Part 01

Definition

Service levels are the measures of how quickly support answers and resolves a ticket. They are first response time, specialist queue wait and resolution within the service level agreement (SLA). Customer satisfaction rates the ticket instead, so it falls outside the subject.

Part 02

Subtopics

Each measure starts and stops at its own events, so a ticket can be fast on one and late on another.

First response time

The clock runs from a ticket's creation to the first reply a person sends, and counts every hour, nights and weekends included. It measures how long a customer goes without hearing from anyone.

Resolution within SLA

Each ticket gets a due time by channel from the SLA policy.S1 A ticket resolved before its due time counts as resolved within SLA, however long its first reply took.

  • ReportQ3 speed and service levels

Specialist queue wait

The wait starts when Tier 1 escalates a ticket and ends when a specialist picks it up. It falls inside the time a ticket has before its due time, so a long wait can make the ticket late.

Part 05

Examples

The example follows one ticket through all three waits. The ticket and its times are illustrative.

Large refund on chat

A chat opens at 10:00 about a $3,000 refund, and Tier 1 replies and escalates it at 10:04. First response time is 4m, and the due time is 14:00, since chat counts every hour.S1

A billing specialist picks the ticket up at 11:00, a specialist queue wait of 56m. The refund is above the $2,500 a specialist can approve, so it needs the billing operations lead and finance sign-off.S4

That approval can take up to 2 business days, and here it arrives the next morning.S4 The ticket misses its due time despite a fast first reply and a short specialist wait.
