Part 01

Definition

Support quality pillars are four sides of support quality, each judged by measures of its own: speed, accuracy, effort and sentiment. Support Operations uses them to choose and audit the measures in its reviews.

Part 03

Rationale

A review that reports one side of quality invites a gain on that side at a cost to the others.S1

A fast first reply that settles nothing raises speed and lowers sentiment. A long first contact that settles everything does the reverse. With a measure from every pillar in one review, the cost of a gain sits beside the gain.

Part 04

Pillars

Each pillar names the measures a review reports for it.

Framework

Support quality pillars

Four pillars cover the quality a customer receives, and no one pillar stands in for another.

  • Speed

    How soon a person answers and the ticket closes, from first response time and resolved within SLA.

  • Accuracy

    Whether the first fix holds, read from the share of tickets reopened after close.

  • Effort

    How much work one issue costs the customer, counted as contacts per issue.

  • Sentiment

    How the customer rates the help, as customer satisfaction (CSAT) from the survey sent after close.

Use when
  • Choosing the measures for a quarterly or team review.
  • Auditing a review or dashboard that reports one pillar.
Not for
  • Rating one agent's work, which coaching reviews cover.
  • Planning staff numbers, which the workforce plan covers.
Part 05

Application

A review applies the framework by taking one measure per pillar and reporting the four together.

  1. Name the review and the decision it informs.
  2. Pick one measure for each pillar from those the pillar names.
  3. Confirm that each measure has a source, an owner and a target.
  4. Report the four measures side by side, for the same period and scope.
  5. Name a pillar with no measure as a gap in the review.
Part 06

Limits

The framework names what to measure, not how to weigh the pillars against one another.

Pillars that move apart

When speed improves and sentiment falls, the framework shows both and ranks neither. The review's owner decides which pillar matters more for the decision at hand, and says so in the review.

Late and partial sentiment

The survey goes out 24 hours after close, so sentiment trails the other pillars. In Q3 2026, 21.1% of resolved tickets returned a survey, so the score speaks for about a fifth of them.S2

Part 08

Examples

Each example applies the four pillars to a review: one to audit it, one to choose its measures.

Q3 2026 operations review

The Q3 review reports speed through first response time and resolved within SLA, and sentiment through CSAT.S2 It reports no reopen rate and no contacts per issue, so accuracy and effort are gaps. So its finding that billing specialists are the lowest-performing team rests on two pillars of four.

Billing specialists' team review

A team review for the billing specialists takes resolved within SLA, reopen rate, contacts per issue and CSAT. Q3 gives values for two of them: 82.6% within SLA and 83.9% CSAT.S2 The review names accuracy and effort as gaps until a source reports them.
