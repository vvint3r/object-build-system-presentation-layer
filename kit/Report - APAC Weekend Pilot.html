Part 01

Executive summary

With two volunteer agents a shift on APAC weekends, the Monday queue fell 55% over four weekends. Weekend tickets got a first reply within two hours, but resolution did not speed up, and Wednesday first response slowed 27 minutes.S1

Summary

Key findings

  1. The Monday queue fell 55% with weekend cover. Tickets waiting at Monday open fell from 212 to 96 on average, and the Monday median first response fell 45 minutes.S1
  2. Weekend tickets got a first reply within two hours. The weekend median first response fell from 29h 40m to 1h 50m.S1
  3. Weekend resolution stayed inside the no-change line. The median fell 4%, from 46h 10m to 44h 30m, while billing tickets still waited for Monday.S1S5
  4. Wednesday first response got 27 minutes slower. Most volunteers took a rest day on Wednesday, and the Wednesday median rose from 2h 24m to 2h 51m.S1S2
  5. Four weekends with no control region leave the size of the effect uncertain. Three other changes in or near the two periods could each flatter the result.S3S1
Part 02

Situation

Until the pilot began on 16 September, APAC had no staffed weekend coverage, so tickets created from Friday evening waited for Monday.S3 Monday mornings opened with a queue of weekend tickets ahead of the day’s new ones.

In the four weekends before the pilot, an average of 212 tickets waited at Monday open. Weekend tickets waited a median 29h 40m for a first reply.S1

Part 03

Intervention

From the first pilot weekend on 19 September, two volunteer agents covered each APAC weekend shift, 08:00 to 20:00 local time on Saturday and Sunday.S2 They worked no overtime, and most took a weekend day back on Wednesday.S2

Part 05

Confounds

In or near the two periods, three other changes could each make the pilot look better than it was.

  • Billing v4 launched on 12 August, ten days before the first period, and raised billing contacts by 31%.S3 A busier before period would overstate the queue’s fall.
  • The macro library rewrite of 26 August fell inside the before period.S3 Some of the after period’s gain may come from agents growing used to the new macros.
  • APAC weekend volume fell 3%, from 1,040 to 1,010 tickets.S1 Thirty fewer tickets over four weekends explain at most about 8 of the Monday queue’s fall of 116.

Appendix A

Methodology

A.1

Terms used in this report
Definitions
Monday queue
APAC Tier 1 tickets waiting for an agent at 09:00 local time each Monday, averaged over the four Mondays of a period.
First response
Elapsed time from a ticket’s creation to the first human reply, as First response time defines it. Monday, Wednesday and weekend medians cover tickets created on those days, local time.
Weekend resolution
Median time from creation to solve for APAC tickets created on a Saturday or Sunday, whatever day they were solved.
No change
A move of less than 5% of the before value, in either direction. A move of 5% or more counts as better or worse.
A.2

Data sources and coverage
  • APAC Tier 1 tickets created 22 August to 14 September and 19 September to 12 October 2026, local time.
  • Spam and merged duplicates are left out, with the filters of the Q3 review.

Limitations

  • Four weekends each side
    Each period holds four Mondays, three Wednesdays and eight weekend days, so one unusual day moves a median. The before period cannot start before the Billing v4 launch.    OpenFix due 2027-01-08
  • No control region
    Only APAC lacked weekend cover, so no region could serve as a control, and the season cannot be separated from the pilot.    By design
  • Volunteer rota
    Volunteers chose to work weekends, and a permanent rota may draw less experienced agents.    Open
