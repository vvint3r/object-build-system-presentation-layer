Part 01

Kit usage

Every report is one HTML file built from the classes in cm-report.css, with behaviour from cm-report.js and charts drawn by ECharts. Agents compose; they never style. An agent that needs a block this gallery lacks uses the nearest one and flags the gap.

cm-report.css

Tokens for light, dark and print, plus every component class.

cm-report.js

Theme toggle, chart theme and draw-in, sortable tables, contents scroll-spy, print prep.

template.html

The starter every report copies. Placeholders in {{ }}.

SPEC.md

The same rules written for agents, as hard rules, budgets and canonical markup.

Linked package

The report links report-kit/cm-report.css and report-kit/cm-report.js. Use when reports are published to one site or folder so a kit update reaches every report.

Self-contained file

Paste the full CSS into one <style> and the full JS into one <script> at the end of <body>. Fonts and ECharts stay on CDN links. Use when the report is emailed or attached.

Kept from Foundations

Three rules for text, accent and emphasis

Text is black or white: focus is carried by weight 300 → 900, never by grey.
Accent is never text: teal appears only as bars, underlines, borders, chips, chart marks and the reserved shadow.
One emphasis per view: the teal offset shadow marks one element per report, the recommendations.

Part 04

Report anatomy

Blocks appear in this order; optional blocks are left out, not filled with placeholders. Required blocks always appear, except in a document without measured numbers. It may leave out a KPI strip, period or Recommendations section, with an omission comment saying why (SPEC.md, section 7).

Part 05

Components

Each block shows its rule, a live example, and the exact markup to copy. Text in the markup is sample copy; structure and class names are fixed.

5.1

Section header

A “Part 01” kicker in weight 300, the T2 title, and an optional TL;DR that states the section’s finding. The kit adds the slash, and a 4px rule closes the header across the full column. Appendices use “Appendix A”.

Two to four KPIs; add cm-kpis--3 or cm-kpis--2 when there are fewer than four. The delta always names its comparison, and an optional sparkline carries no axis. cm-delta--good and --bad describe the movement for the reader, not its direction; neutral uses plain cm-delta.

5.4

Callouts

Icon and label on one row, the body directly under the label. No fill: the label’s underline carries the semantic color and the icon tells the types apart. The label set is closed at eight, and more than two callouts in a row become a list.

Note
Context the reader needs to read the figure correctly.
Tip
A practical shortcut or better method.
Important
The one thing not to miss. Use once per section at most.
Warning
A condition that will produce an incorrect result if it is not accounted for.
Example
Worked instance: 412 of 437 runs on time → 94.3%.
Requirement
A rule the reader must follow. REQ-04
Limitation
What the data cannot show.
Correction
Previously 91.8%, now 91.2%: state the old and new values and the cause.
5.5

Definition

Explains a term the reader needs before the numbers make sense, placed where it first matters or in Appendix A.1 with the full set. The term is in 800 above its explanation; a dotted rule separates the icon from the block. Label “Definition” for one term, “Definitions” for several; add an example when the definition alone is abstract.

Definitions
Backlog
Tickets in open or pending state at the snapshot time. Tickets waiting on the customer count as pending. Example: 1,186 tickets open at Sunday 23:59 UTC.
CSAT
Share of survey responses scoring 4 or 5 on a 5-point scale, weighted by response count.
5.6

Questions

Answers the questions readers ask about a section, in their words. The question is in 800; the answer states the number or the decision first. Two to five per block, at the end of the section they belong to.

Questions
Why did the backlog rise in week 7?
Billing v4 launched on 12 August and billing contacts rose 31%. The backlog peaked at 1,710 that week and fell every week after.
Does SLA attainment include weekends?
For chat and phone, yes; both run around the clock. Email and web-form SLAs count business hours only.

5.8

Inline emphasis

A light semantic wash behind a phrase, 1px smaller than the text around it and at the same weight. Five roles plus the dotted concept underline for a term’s first use, which links never take. Bold marks a phrase, never a sentence.

Trend alerts produced a +1.8 pp recovery, confirmed by the geo-holdout re-run.

The legacy collector still under-reports, so cuts stay directional only. Hold the 98% target against the new freshness SLO.

5.9

Pull quote

For the verbatim words of a customer, an interviewee or a stakeholder. One per section at most. Bold the decisive phrase; attribution is always present.

Chat had my invoice corrected before I finished my coffee. The same fix over email took two days.

Customer, mid-market planCSAT verbatim · September 2026

A native table, with kind and title above the surface and the source below. Columns are split by 16px gutters so no rule runs unbroken, and numbers are right-aligned with .num. A totals row in <tfoot> sits under a dotted rule with extra space above it.

Status color goes in the row flag (data-flag on the row), a bar in the left gutter; the status word stays in the row. Add data-sortable for click-to-sort.

5.18

Recommendations card

Holds the actions the report asks the reader to take. It is the only block in the Recommendations section, which follows the body sections and precedes the appendices. Each action is a bold instruction, one sentence of reason, then owner, due date and expected impact.

It never opens a report; the executive summary does. It carries the reserved L2 treatment, black border plus teal offset, exactly once per report. It fits its content, between 520px and the reading measure, so short actions give a narrow card.

Part 06

Charts

Every chart is an ECharts option written as JSON inside a .cm-chart. The kit applies the theme: Nunito labels, a 1.5px ink baseline, hairline gridlines, thin rounded bars, a bordered tooltip and dashed ink target lines. Agents write data and structure only.

Chart rules

  • One message per chart: the figure title states what it shows; the prose above states the finding.
  • Color by role: the focal series takes $c1, the comparison $c2 or $context. More than four series becomes small multiples or a table.
  • Legend for two or more series: added automatically, top left, in series order. End labels and value labels may be added; they never replace the legend.
  • Axis titles carry units: name the value axis (“Tickets per week”) unless every tick already shows the unit (“90%”).
  • Bars and areas start at zero: lines may zoom.
  • No 3D, gradient fills or dual y-axes: pie or donut only for part-to-whole with four slices or fewer.
  • Text alternative: every chart has an aria-label stating the type and the finding, and a source line.

6.1

Line: focus against context

The current period in $c1; prior period in $context. The legend appears on its own; end labels repeat the series names at the line ends. An event marker is a vertical markLine.

6.2

Bar: ranking with a target

Horizontal bars for ranked categories, sorted with the largest at the top. Value labels on, target as a dashed markLine. One series, so no legend.

6.3

Stacked columns: composition

Up to four parts. The kit separates segments with a thin surface gap and rounds every segment.

6.4

Area: one level over time

A single series with "areaStyle": {}. Thresholds as markLine on the y-axis.

6.5

Scatter: relationship

Name both axes with units. Highlight one point by giving it $c1 and a label; the rest stay $c2.

6.6

Donut: part of a whole

Four slices or fewer, labelled with names and values, which act as the legend. Use only when the total itself matters.

6.7

Heatmap: two categories and a magnitude

Sequential ramp via "inRange": {"color": "$seq"} (applied by default), cells separated by a 3px $surface border. The scale bar under the grid is the legend; give its ends values with text.

Part 07

Motion, dark mode, print

Motion

Charts only

  • Each chart draws in once, the first time 15% of it is on screen.
  • 900ms ease-out; series stagger by 120ms.
  • Target and event lines appear without animation.
  • Nothing else moves except the hover lift on interactive surfaces.
  • Reduced-motion and print draw charts static.
Dark mode

Token swap, no markup

  • Follows the reader’s system setting; the toggle stores their choice.
  • Text turns white; surfaces step up from #0d0d0d to #171717.
  • Accent brightens to #2fd4a8; chart colors lift a step.
  • Charts redraw from the dark tokens.
Print / PDF

Always light

  • Top bar, rail and buttons hidden; 16 × 14mm margins.
  • Soft shadows become 1px borders; the L2 card keeps its offset.
  • Side-by-side figures marked cm-print-stack stack full width.
  • Figures, KPIs and callouts never split across pages.
  • cm-section--break forces a page break.
Part 08

Agent checklist

An agent checks each item before publishing. A report that fails any item is not published.

Structure and evidence

  1. Started from template.html; no class, color or font outside the kit.
  2. No inline style except --h, --v, --t and --steps.
  3. Masthead complete with title, dek, status, period, published date, reading time, version and byline; an empty period is left out with its omission comment.
  4. Executive summary has a lede, 2 to 4 KPIs with stated comparisons, and 3 to 5 findings. Without measured numbers, the KPI strip is left out with its omission comment.
  5. Every number in the summary appears again, with its source, in the body.
  6. Every figure has a kind, title, description, legend when there are 2+ series, axis titles with units, aria-label and source line.
  7. Every table has a kind and title, .num on numeric columns, and a source caption.
  8. One recommendations card (none, with its omission comment, when there are no actions), at most one solid verdict chip per table, one summary box.

Style, links and writing

  1. Accent never used as text color; no grey text; mono only for code.
  2. Text before a colon that leads a sentence, list item or caption is bold.
  3. Nothing closer than 24px to the block next to it.
  4. Footnote links resolve both ways; sources list every dataset used.
  5. Contents rail links match section ids. Checked in light, dark and print preview.
  6. Prose within the limits of SPEC.md section 13, in plain words and neutral third person, with no em or en dashes. Each section leads with its finding; headings are plain noun phrases in sentence case.
