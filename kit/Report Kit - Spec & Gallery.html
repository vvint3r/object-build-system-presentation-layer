Part 01

Kit usage

Every document is one HTML file built from the classes in cm-report.css, with behaviour from cm-report.js and charts drawn by ECharts. Agents compose; they never style. An agent that needs a block this gallery lacks uses the nearest one and flags the gap.

cm-report.css

Tokens for light, dark and print, plus every component class.

cm-report.js

Theme toggle, chart theme and draw-in, sortable tables, contents scroll-spy, link targets clear of the top bar, print prep.

template.html

The starter every report copies; guides, references and each page type copy their own, such as template-guide.html. Placeholders in {{ }}.

SPEC.md

The same rules written for agents, as hard rules, budgets and canonical markup.

Linked package

The report links report-kit/cm-report.css and report-kit/cm-report.js. Use when reports are published to one site or folder so a kit update reaches every report.

Self-contained file

Paste the full CSS into one <style> and the full JS into one <script> at the end of <body>. Fonts and ECharts stay on CDN links. Use when the report is emailed or attached.

Offline file

Run npm run pack on a finished document in the kit's repository. It writes one file to offline/ with the CSS, JS, fonts and ECharts inside. Use when the report is read with no network.

Kept from Foundations

Three rules for text, accent and emphasis

Text is black or white

Weight from 300 to 900 carries focus, never grey.

Accent is never text

Teal appears only as bars, underlines, borders, chips, chart marks and the reserved shadow.

One emphasis per view

The teal offset shadow marks one element per report, the recommendations.

Part 04

Document anatomy

The kit builds three kinds of document, each with its own required parts in a fixed order. All three share the top bar, contents rail, masthead, Notes & sources and footer.

A cm-kind tag in the head, after cm-kit, names the kind: report, guide or reference. A page without one is a report.

Components

Each block shows its rule, a live example, and the exact markup to copy. Text in the markup is sample copy; structure and class names are fixed.

A “Part 01” kicker in weight 300, the T2 title, and an optional TL;DR that states the section’s finding under its own label. The kit adds the slash, and a 4px rule closes the header across the full column. Appendices use “Appendix A”.

5.4

Callouts

Icon and label on one row, the body directly under the label. No fill: the label’s underline carries the semantic color and the icon tells the types apart. The label set is closed at eight, and more than two callouts in a row become a list.

5.5

Definition

Explains a term the reader needs before the numbers make sense, placed where it first matters or in Appendix A.1 with the full set. The term is in 800 above its explanation; a dotted rule separates the icon from the block. Label “Definition” for one term, “Definitions” for several; add an example sentence when the definition alone is abstract.

Definitions
Backlog
Tickets in open or pending state at the snapshot time. Tickets waiting on the customer count as pending. At Sunday 23:59 UTC, 1,186 tickets were open.
CSAT
Share of survey responses scoring 4 or 5 on a 5-point scale, weighted by response count.
5.6

Questions

Answers the questions readers ask about a section, in their words. The question is in 800; the answer states the number or the decision first. Two to five per block, at the end of the section they belong to.

Questions

Billing v4 launched on 12 August and billing contacts rose 31%. The backlog peaked at 1,710 that week and fell every week after.
Does SLA attainment include weekends?
For chat and phone, yes; both run around the clock. Email, web-form and social SLAs count business hours only.

A light semantic wash behind a phrase, 1px smaller than the text around it and at the same weight. Five roles plus the dotted concept underline for a term’s first use, which links never take. Bold marks a phrase, never a sentence.

For the verbatim words of a customer, an interviewee or a stakeholder. One per section at most. Bold the decisive phrase; attribution is always present.

5.18

Recommendations card

Holds the actions the report asks the reader to take. It opens the Recommendations section, which follows the body sections and precedes the appendices; at most one callout follows it. Each action is an instruction on a line of its own, then one sentence of reason, then owner, date and, when estimated, expected impact.

It never opens a report; the executive summary does. It carries the reserved L2 treatment, black border plus teal offset, exactly once per report. It fits its content, between 520px and the reading measure, so short actions give a narrow card.

5.28

Workflow

How work moves between roles: stages in order, each with the role that does it and the condition that hands the work on. The stages share the numbered list of the procedure (5.26), which holds the steps one reader carries out.

The process diagram (5.12) gives the same stages at a glance. A flow diagram (7.1, 7.2) draws the branches and lanes; the workflow is the reference a reader works from.

5.29

Framework

Names the parts that together cover a subject, each with what it covers. Peers sit two or three across; layers go in an ordered list, which stacks them top first.

When every part carries the same attributes and the reader compares across them, the parts belong in a table (5.10).

Framework

Support quality pillars

Four pillars cover the quality a customer receives, and no one pillar stands in for another.

  • Speed

    How soon a person answers and the ticket closes, from first response time and resolved within SLA.

  • Accuracy

    Whether the first fix holds, read from the share of tickets reopened after close.

  • Effort

    How much work one issue costs the customer, counted as contacts per issue.

  • Sentiment

    How the customer rates the help, as customer satisfaction (CSAT) from the survey sent after close.

Use when
  • Choosing the measures for a quarterly or team review.
  • Auditing a review or dashboard that reports one pillar.
Not for
  • Rating one agent's work, which coaching reviews cover.
  • Planning staff numbers, which the workforce plan covers.
5.30

Model

A named abstraction a reader carries away and applies: its claim, its parts, and the conditions under which it holds and breaks. Without the breaking condition it is a diagram with a name.

A model that sorts things by two factors adds a two by two grid. Each cell names its position, so the grid reads the same stacked on a phone.

Model

Ticket placement model

The queue that resolves a ticket is set by two needs: product depth and account access.

  • Product depth

    How far into the product a fix reaches, from a help-centre answer to a code change.

  • Account access

    Whether the fix changes billing or account records past the Tier 1 limits, which only specialists may change.

  1. Shallow · no access

    Tier 1

    Answered from macros and the help centre.

  2. Shallow · access

    Billing specialists

    Large refunds, proration and invoice corrections.

  3. Deep · no access

    Technical escalations

    Reproduced and fixed through configuration.

  4. Deep · access

    Engineering

    Data repairs that need a code change.

Holds when
  • Triage can tell the intent before an agent picks the ticket up.
Breaks when
  • One ticket holds a billing and a technical question, so it is split before placement.
5.31

Method

A repeatable technique: what it achieves, when it applies, its steps and the decision it ends in. The decision is a condition, never a question.

Work passed between roles is a workflow (5.28); an action taken once, with an owner and a date, is a recommendation (5.18).

Method

SLA breach review

The review finds the causes behind a week's SLA breaches and gives each large cause an owner.

Run by
Team lead
Needs
Breached-ticket export and the cause list
Cadence
Weekly, on Monday
Use when
  • A team's weekly SLA attainment falls below its target.
Not for
  • A single escalation, which the incident review covers.
Steps
  1. Export the week's breached tickets with their queue and intent.
  2. Tag each ticket with one cause from the cause list.
  3. Count tickets by cause and sort the causes largest first.
  4. Check the largest causes against the change log for a release that week.
  5. Record each large cause with its owner and a fix date in the review note.
Decision

A cause behind a fifth or more of the breaches gets an owner and a fix date. Smaller causes are logged and counted again next week.

5.33

Evaluation

Scores one subject against weighted criteria and ends in a verdict. The weighted score is each score times its weight, over the sum of the weights, on the criteria's scale.

The verdict is a status chip: Adopt, Hold or Reject. Several options scored side by side go in a comparison table (5.10).

Part 06

Charts

Every chart is an ECharts option written as JSON inside a .cm-chart. The kit applies the theme: Nunito labels, a 1.5px ink baseline, hairline gridlines, thin rounded bars, a bordered tooltip and dashed ink target lines. Agents write data and structure only.

Chart rules

  • Each chart carries one message: the figure title states what it shows, and the prose above states the finding.
  • Color follows role: the focal series takes $c1, the comparison $c2 or $context. More than four series become small multiples or a table.
  • Two or more series get a legend, added at the top left in series order. A slope chart, or a line chart whose end labels name every line, has none.
  • Axis titles carry units: the value axis is named (“Tickets per week”) unless every tick already shows the unit (“90%”).
  • Bars and areas start at zero; lines may zoom.
  • No chart uses 3D, gradient fills or dual y-axes. A pie or donut shows only a part of a whole, in four slices or fewer.
  • Every chart has an aria-label stating its type and finding, and a source line.

6.2

Line: focus against context

The current period in $c1; prior period in $context. End labels with "{a}" name both lines where they end, so the kit leaves the legend out, and moves labels apart that would touch. An event marker is a vertical markLine.

6.3

Bar: ranking with a target

Horizontal bars for ranked categories, sorted with the largest at the top. Value labels on, target as a dashed markLine. One series, so no legend.

6.4

Stacked columns: composition

Up to four parts. The kit separates segments with a thin surface gap and rounds every segment.

6.5

Area: one level over time

A single series with "areaStyle": {}. Thresholds as markLine on the y-axis.

6.6

Scatter: relationship

Name both axes with units. Highlight one point by giving it $c1 and a label; the rest stay $c2.

6.7

Donut: part of a whole

Four slices or fewer, labelled with names and values, which act as the legend. Use only when the total itself matters.

6.8

Heatmap: two categories and a magnitude

Sequential ramp via "inRange": {"color": "$seq"} (applied by default), cells separated by a 3px $surface border. The scale bar under the grid is the legend; give its ends values with text.

6.9

Bullet: each measure against its target

A horizontal bar series with "cm": {"chart": "bullet"}. The kit draws a grey track per bar, the target as an ink mark across it, and each value at the row's end. Optional bands grade the scale in up to three grey steps, named above the top row.

6.10

Slope: two points in time

A line chart with two categories and "cm": {"chart": "slope"} at the top level. Labels at both ends name each line and give its value, so there is no legend. Lines are ink; a line given "color": "$c1" stands out and the rest turn grey.

6.11

Interval band: a forecast with its range

A line series with "cm": {"chart": "interval"}, a low and a high per point, and the interval's name. The kit fills the band in the line's colour and names it in the legend.

6.12

Strip: every value

A scatter series with "cm": {"chart": "strip"} and every value per category. The kit spreads the dots across each row, marks the median and writes the count under each name.

6.13

Box: quartiles and outliers

A boxplot series with "cm": {"chart": "box"} and the same values. The kit computes the quartiles, draws whiskers to 1.5 times the interquartile range and the values past them as dots. With summaries only, give five numbers per category and the counts in n.

6.14

Error bars: estimates per category

A scatter or bar series with "cm": {"chart": "interval"}. The kit draws an ink error bar on each point, names the interval in the legend and moves each value label past it. Points read better than bars when only the estimate matters.

Part 07

Diagrams

A diagram is data written as JSON inside a .cm-diagram, as a chart is. The author places each node on a column and a row and names the edges. The kit draws the shapes, the lines and their labels, and a text version.

Diagram rules

  • A flow diagram shows branches, loops or lanes. Three to six steps in a line are a process diagram.
  • Shape marks the node type and line style the edge kind. A key names the rarer ones.
  • Every branch out of a decision carries a label: yes and no, or the condition.
  • A branch that ends early ends at a terminal of its own, named for the outcome.
  • col counts across and row down, whichever way the flow reads.
  • An edge label stands on its line, near where the line leaves its node, with line on both sides.
  • One node at most is emphasised, with a chip naming why.
  • The kit writes a text version under the caption, and a summary when the aria-label is empty.

Size and print

  • At most 24 nodes, 5 columns (4 with lanes), 5 rows, 5 lanes and 12 edge labels; a labelled gap counts as a column. A larger flow splits into an overview and details.
  • A diagram too wide for its figure or the printed page takes the narrow layout and scales to fit, down to 80%. Its text is set larger, so none draws under 11px.

7.1

Process and decision flow

Steps in reading order, a decision with labelled branches, and a loop back for rework. The empty aria-label lets the kit write the summary.

7.2

Swimlane

One lane per role, each a row band named on the left. A step sits in the lane of the role that does it, so every hand-off crosses a lane line.

7.3

Sequence

Messages between people or systems in the order they are sent, with time running down. Order places everything, so the JSON has no columns or rows.

7.4

State

The states one ticket passes through, and the event behind each change. A dot marks the start, a ringed dot the end, and a loop an event that leaves the state as it was.

7.5

Entity relationship

The records a helpdesk keeps and how they relate. Tags mark the keys, and the words beside each end of a line give the count at that end.

7.6

Journey map

Phases as frames across the top, one step per column under them, and the experience line under the steps. Each step carries its level on the scale the experience list names, lowest first. Each word stands under its run, between that level and the next one down.

7.7

System and data flow

Systems and the data that moves between them: boundaries frame the systems, stores carry a rule above and below, and external entities a heavy border. Dotted edges move data and solid edges pass control. Every edge that crosses a boundary is named.

7.8

Layer stack

Layers from the consumer at the top to the source at the base, each named on the left, with its components placed by column. A dependency runs down, from the component that depends to the one it depends on. At most 5 layers of 4 components.

7.9

Hierarchy

A tree read left to right: each node names its parent, and children appear in the order written. The kit places the levels and draws one line from each parent to each child. At most 4 levels and 6 leaves.

Part 08

Motion, dark mode, print

Motion

Charts only

  • Each chart draws in once, the first time 15% of it is on screen.
  • 900ms ease-out; series stagger by 120ms.
  • Target and event lines appear without animation.
  • Nothing else moves except the hover lift on interactive surfaces.
  • Reduced-motion and print draw charts static.
Dark mode

Token swap, no markup

  • Follows the reader’s system setting; the toggle stores their choice.
  • Text turns white; surfaces step up from #0d0d0d to #171717.
  • Accent brightens to #2fd4a8; chart colors lift a step.
  • Charts redraw from the dark tokens.
Print / PDF

Always light

  • Top bar, rail and buttons hidden; 16 × 14mm margins.
  • Soft shadows become 1px borders; the L2 card keeps its offset.
  • Side-by-side figures marked cm-print-stack stack full width.
  • Figures, KPIs and callouts never split across pages.
  • cm-section--break forces a page break.
  • Diagrams scale to fit the column, never below 80%, with their text set larger to print at its own size.
  • Tables hold to the column with 12px gutters; no text prints under 11px.
Part 09

Agent checklist

An agent checks each item before publishing. A document that fails any item is not published.

Structure and evidence

  1. Started from its kind's or type's template; no class, color or font outside the kit.
  2. No inline style except --h, --v, --t and --steps.
  3. Masthead complete with title, dek, type and status chips, published date, reading time, version and byline. A report adds its period.
  4. A report's summary has a lede, 2 to 4 KPIs with comparisons and 3 to 5 findings. Other kinds hold their parts in order.
  5. A required block left out carries its omission comment.
  6. Every number in the summary appears again, with its source, in the body.
  7. Every figure has a kind, title, description, legend or end labels (2+ series), axis titles with units, aria-label and source line.
  8. Every table has a kind and title, .num on numeric columns, and a source caption.
  9. A report has one summary box and one recommendations card; guides and references have neither. At most one solid verdict chip per table.

Style, links and writing

  1. Accent never used as text color; no grey text; mono only for code.
  2. No bold label or opening bold phrase runs into its text; a caption’s source and note lines are lines of their own.
  3. Nothing closer than 24px to the block next to it.
  4. Footnotes and citations link both ways; every source has a key, type, locator and date, and the sources list every dataset used.
  5. Changing numbers sit in one dated status block; change entries are dated, newest first.
  6. Contents rail links match section ids. Checked in light, dark and print preview.
  7. An id in the head, a breadcrumb from set to document, and a passing link check.
  8. Prose within the limits of SPEC.md section 13, in plain words and neutral third person, with no em or en dashes. Each section leads with its finding; headings are plain noun phrases in sentence case.
